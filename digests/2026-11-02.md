# DBMS Weekly — 2026-11-02 (week of Nov 2–8)

_Days before 19 RC2, Andrew Dunstan reverted the `to_json`/`to_jsonb` immutability change after it let JSON expression indexes go stale; anything built on 19rc1 that depends on it must be redefined before dump/restore or pg_upgrade. The 18.7/17.12/16.16/15.20/14.25 notes collect two checkpoint-race fixes, Memoize and Sort wrong results, a pg_trgm GiST fix that needs a REINDEX, and a walsender lost wakeup. AI-found bugs became the list's meta-story, from a tester holding over 1,000 to Peter Geoghegan's LLM-written rival to the nbtree prefix-compression patch. On the blogs failover dominated, alongside UUIDv4's real insert cost, a TOAST page-size trap and PostHog leaving DuckDB._

## PostgreSQL

- **[UUIDv4 vs UUIDv7: what a random primary key costs in Postgres](https://postgres.ai/blog/20261105-uuidv4-vs-uuidv7)** — on 18.6, once the primary key outgrew shared_buffers (~26 M rows), UUIDv4 inserts ran at 0.62–0.69× an ordered key and wrote 1.5–1.9× the WAL per row; generate v7 server-side. *(Nikolay Samokhvalov · postgres.ai)*
- **[Postgres logical replication beyond one writer](https://tapoueh.org/blog/2026/11/postgres-logical-replication-beyond-one-writer/)** — four regions of pg_auto_failover groups linked only by core logical replication: a planned failover lost nothing, `kill -9` gave 2–4 duplicates and no holes, emptied `synchronized_standby_slots` lost 3–13 rows; DDL remains the gap. *(Dimitri Fontaine · tapoueh.org)* `[by pg_auto_failover / pgcopydb author]`
- **[Fencing a Postgres primary that might still be alive](https://clickhouse.com/blog/fencing-a-postgres-primary-that-might-still-be-alive)** — promotion alone doesn't stop an old primary that a session can switch to `synchronous_commit = local`, so Ubicloud cuts its network (host veth, force-detached AWS NIC, deleted GCP NIC) and promotes anyway if every fence fails. *(Furkan Sahin · clickhouse.com / Ubicloud)* `[vendor blog — substantive]`
- **[Designing a reliable unplanned failover](https://planetscale.com/blog/designing-a-reliable-unplanned-failover)** — the opposite answer: no lease; Neki fails over only when a reachable replica reports broken replication and none contradicts it, with keepalives and `wal_receiver_timeout` tuned so crashes, drops and freezes look alike. *(Shlomi Noach · planetscale.com)* `[vendor blog — substantive]`
- **[PostgreSQL document compression: TOAST thresholds, storage policies, and page size](https://dev.to/franckpachot/postgresql-document-compression-toast-thresholds-storage-policies-and-page-size-oh4)** — TOAST tries compression only past a hardcoded quarter-page gate that `toast_tuple_target` can't lower, so on 16/32 KB builds of 19rc1 3.9 KB and 7.7 KB JSONB documents stop compressing. *(Franck Pachot · dev.to)*
- **[Patroni, Ubuntu 24.04, and two unplanned PostgreSQL failovers](https://dev.to/franckpachot/patroni-ubuntu-2404-and-two-unplanned-postgresql-failovers-52gl)** — unattended-upgrades updated zlib, needrestart restarted the custom Patroni unit, and with `KillMode=control-group` the stop signal reached Postgres too: two failovers in a day, traced through journal and dpkg logs. *(Franck Pachot · dev.to)*
- **[systemd-sysusers and directory-managed Postgres accounts](https://enterprisedb.com/blog/systemd-sysusers-and-directory-managed-postgres-accounts-avoiding-startup-failures)** — PGDG RPMs now create `postgres` via sysusers.d at early boot, before AD/LDAP answers, so a directory-managed account gets a local twin with another UID and the cluster fails to start after reboot. *(Nick Ivanov · enterprisedb.com)*
- **[Establishing the PostgreSQL standard: PGConf.dev 2026 part two](https://vyruss.org/blog/establishing-the-postgresql-standard-pgconfdev-2026-part-two.html)** — postgres-compat, a reference test for "Postgres-compatible": Postgres's own 200 regression tests and 117 isolation specs run unchanged against the target, plus 74 probes for silent failures and contrib behaviour. *(Jimmy Angelakos · vyruss.org)*
- **[Building a table access method, part 7: storing and reading rows](https://dbmsdrops.kindahl.net/2026/11/02/building-a-table-access-method-part-7/)** — `pg_memtable` gains INSERT and a full scan over its DSM arena: how slots carry rows into the AM, what a TID must encode, arena allocation, and visibility with two concurrent writers. *(Mats Kindahl · dbmsdrops.kindahl.net)*
- **["server closed the connection unexpectedly" in PostgreSQL](https://now-next.nl/en/insights/postgresql-server-closed-the-connection-unexpectedly/)** — five causes behind libpq's message, each reproduced on 13–18 across six drivers and PgBouncer; no library surfaced the 57P02 crash notice, and an OOM kill logs exactly like `kill -9`. *(Chris van Eijk · now-next.nl)*
- **[A month with WalShadow on ClickHouse Managed Postgres](https://clickhouse.com/blog/a-month-with-walshadow-clickhouse-managed-postgres)** — decoding physical WAL outside Postgres, with no slot, held a 0.37–0.41 s median commit-to-visible latency up to 200,000 rows/s, while logical-replication ClickPipes tracked its 60 s sync interval. *(Hidehiko Asahi · clickhouse.com)* `[vendor blog]` `[unverified: private preview]`
- **[Why I'm maintaining a VectorChord fork](https://vonng.com/en/pg/maintain-vchord/)** and **[pg_mooncake](https://vonng.com/en/pg/fork-pg-mooncake/)** — VectorChord, silent since April, lives on as `pgsty/*` forks for PG 14–18; a pg_mooncake port to pg_duckdb 1.2 went upstream once Mooncake Labs said it would keep maintaining it. *(Ruohang Feng · vonng.com)*
- **[OrioleDB v1.10.1](https://github.com/orioledb/orioledb/releases/tag/v1.10.1)** and **[v1.10.2](https://github.com/orioledb/orioledb/releases/tag/v1.10.2)** — releases drop `betaN` numbering a week after the public beta, with no storage or WAL format change. Fixes cover lost updates under REPEATABLE READ, wrong rows through bridged (GIN etc.) indexes, checkpointer and undo-chain PANICs, and decoding crashes on TOASTed tables. Transaction streaming is now disabled at decoding startup cluster-wide. *(orioledb · GitHub)* `[release]`
- **[pg_duckdb 1.2.0](https://github.com/duckdb/pg_duckdb/releases/tag/v1.2.0)** — moves to DuckDB 2.0.0 and is explicitly marked experimental, "not ready for production use", over unresolved user reports. Adds initial PostgreSQL 19 support, `INSERT INTO <pg table> SELECT …` through DuckDB, and installation into a non-public schema. *(duckdb · GitHub)* `[release]`
- **[psqlODBC 18.00.0006](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/179365270066.2811375.7894828225414660033%40wrigleys.postgresql.org)** — a security release that "fixes multiple security issues"; the announcement points only to the changelog. *(pgsql-announce)* `[release]` `[unverified: individual issues not read]`
- **[AgensGraph 2.18](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/179405311893.595140.7019528685237923374%40wrigleys.postgresql.org)** — rebased on PostgreSQL 18.6, it adds the first ISO GQL clauses (`LET`, `FOR`, `FILTER`, `NEXT`, `FINISH`) and graph subqueries. Variable-length traversals now use the edge label's index at each hop; a two-hop query that timed out at 20 s takes milliseconds. *(pgsql-announce)* `[release]` `[unverified: self-reported]`
- **[pgBadger 13.3](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/179371934489.94463.15568227632010632627%40wrigleys.postgresql.org)** — no longer counts a query twice when both `log_min_duration_statement` and `auto_explain` log it. Also parses auto_explain's query parameters, fixes multiline statements and `--dbuser` filtering, and uses per-run temp files so concurrent runs don't collide. *(pgsql-announce)* `[release]`
- **[HypoPG 1.4.5](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/179364934779.2811376.16172441516340286202%40wrigleys.postgresql.org)** — fixes relid handling in hypothetical indexes that could crash the backend, and a long-standing thinko in `hypo_estimate_index`. *(pgsql-announce)* `[release]`
- **[Brume 2.1](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/179405309462.595141.455056588245268087%40wrigleys.postgresql.org)** — an Apache-2.0 tool that copies a subset of a database for testing with personal data pseudonymised. Format-preserving encryption rewrites primary keys and the foreign keys pointing at them, so referential integrity holds without disabling triggers. *(pgsql-announce)* `[release]`

## Research & cutting edge

- **[Correctness firewalls for synthesized database components](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05173)** — agent-written engine parts go behind boundaries that may change speed but never results: advisory for cardinality, guarded for search, certified for compression; synthesized search beat learned indexes up to 3×. *(Amiri, Binnig, Kipf, Kraska, Marcus et al.)* _[paper]_
- **[GenDB-V: AI-generated and formally verified database engines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02779)** — per-query code ships with proofs against a fixed SQL semantics and runs 22–61× faster than DuckDB and Umbra, while a test-only LLM baseline was wrong on 16 of 28 templates. *(Lao, Trummer)* _[paper]_ `[unverified: template-specialised speedups]`
- **[When is one query per template enough? Moving a learned cost model to new hardware](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05892)** — on PostgreSQL in six environments, serial and parallel lines plus one measured query per template reach median Q-error 1.10 (TPC-H) and 1.19 (TPC-DS), near full retraining. *(Patel)* _[paper]_
- **[Fidelity synopses: choosing an ANN index configuration per query](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41407)** — landmark cells record which index configuration met the recall target on sample queries, so the optimizer picks the cheapest one per query in 21–32 µs; median cost 0.72× of static. *(Ramanatha, Singh, Ramanath)* _[paper]_
- **[PQR-Search: per-query recall guarantees for ANN in dynamic vector databases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07579)** — indexes tuned to 0.99 average recall left 5–19% of queries below it; a random rotation inside IVF gives per-query guarantees that survive updates, at 2.5× HNSW latency. *(Loh, Lee)* _[paper]_
- **[Eliminating servers in state machine replication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01363)** — both the total order and the materialised state live on disaggregated storage, with clients sharing one state copy through the log; the AirDB KV store cold-starts 10–45× faster than etcd. *(Park, Hu, Yu, Balakrishnan, Park)* _[paper]_
- **[Quirinus: when deterministic concurrency control beats 2PL and OCC for geo-replication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05882)** — an analytical model of when ordering a batch before execution pays off, then a DCC system reaching up to 6.2× Tiga's TPC-C throughput. *(Mao, Zhang, Zhang, Jacobsen)* _[paper]_ `[unverified: single configuration]`
- **[Recast: storage-resident ANN that handles deletes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02765)** — raw vectors stay on disk and an uncertainty-bounded quantised IVF stays in memory, fetching only candidates that need refinement; batched updates run 3.1–37× faster than SPFresh and OdinANN. *(Yang, Tao, Dong, Chen)* _[paper]_
- **[USDB: ByteDance's sequence storage for recommender systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02618)** — an LSM with time-staged compaction that materialises per-application histories; production migration cut mean write latency 85.8% and infrastructure cost 42.1%. *(Zhu, Cai, Yu, … Chai)* _[paper]_ `[unverified: production self-report]`
- **[Withdrawn: "Teach Your DBMS to LIKE Strings"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23307)** — the TUM Aho-Corasick wildcard-join paper (up to 30.6× over DuckDB) was withdrawn on Nov 2: "The invariant in this manuscript is wrong." *(Nguyen, Ginter, Nguyen, Neumann, Leis)* _[paper]_
