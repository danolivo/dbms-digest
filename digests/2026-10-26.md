# DBMS Weekly — 2026-10-26 (week of Oct 26–Nov 1)

_A family of checkpoint races found by one reporter dominated the lists: ROLLBACK PREPARED never set `DELAY_CHKPT_START`, so a rolled-back prepared transaction can come back after a crash and then be committed, and the same WAL-before-file pattern turns up in multixact creation, CLOG truncation and hash-index bucket allocation. A standby rewound with `pg_rewind` can keep hint bits from its old timeline and disagree with the primary about which rows exist. Fifteen fixes landed in released branches, among them the real fix for the parameterized-Append wrong results from two issues ago, the incremental-backup merge-order data loss, and a TOAST index that went stale across REINDEX CONCURRENTLY. Andres Freund restated his wish to delete hash indexes while Greg Burd posted patches that make them insert as fast as btree and shrink URL btrees by two thirds; the CommitFest's last week before closing took in 64 entries, the most since tracking began. On the blogs, Radim Marek measured what dropping a 4 TB table really costs (the standby freezes replay for the unlink, and the first SELECT after a mass delete wrote 151 GB of WAL on 18), Nikolay Samokhvalov found a 1,400× gap between "restored" and "usable", OrioleDB entered public beta with a public benchmark, Timescale replaced DeltaDelta with six per-block encodings, MariaDB fixed a 3× in-memory regression that dated back to 10.5, ClickHouse shipped a DPsub join orderer with a conflict detector for outer joins, and Jepsen found 96 issues in a database that claimed TLA+ proofs._

## PostgreSQL

- **[Getting rid of a 4 TB table in PostgreSQL](https://boringsql.com/posts/delete-4tb/)** — what DROP, DELETE and the aftermath actually cost, measured on 18.6 with checksums on, three rigs (laptop, Hetzner primary + streaming replica, GCE n2-standard-32 with 16 local NVMe), prompted by a GitLab question at PGConf.EU. *(Radim Marek · boringsql.com)*
    - DROP TABLE of a 9 GB table is 81,737 bytes of WAL; the costs are the lock queue (an AccessExclusiveLock waiter blocks every later SELECT, and dropping an FK child locks the parent too), the Standby/LOCK record that makes the startup process cancel readers after `max_standby_streaming_delay`, and the unlink — on the standby the startup process does it, so **replay stalled for 11 of the 15.9 s** a 192 GB drop took
    - cold unlink on ext4 is ~17 ms per 1 GB segment (1 TB: 18.5 s); the same 1 TB table on XFS dropped in 80 ms, because segments written by 28 parallel appenders had 1,760–4,785 extents on ext4 vs 2–14 on XFS; `vmtouch -e` first brings a 100 GB drop to 0.77 s
    - batched DELETE of 90% of 540 M rows: 36 min, 229 GB of WAL, the replica 38 s behind; then **the first SELECT wrote 151 GB of WAL in 41 minutes** — with checksums (18's default) hint-bit and prune writes log full pages (959,025 `FPI_FOR_HINT`); checksums off cut the laptop's post-delete count(*) from 7,850 MB to 173 MB of WAL, `wal_compression = zstd` to 1,458 MB; copying the 10% keepers into a new table wrote 882 MB
- **[Dump/restore done. Now what?](https://postgres.ai/blog/20261028-dump-restore-now-what)** — a 250 M-row join on 18.6 took **32.6 s right after `pg_restore`** (no statistics: Hash Join over Seq Scan), **6.1 s after ANALYZE** (Index Only Scan with 24,983 heap fetches, since the visibility map is empty) and **23 ms after VACUUM ANALYZE**. `pg_dump --statistics` (18+) imports `relallvisible` for all 4,098,361 pages while the new VM is empty. On 19rc1 the first SELECT itself set 4,098,360 VM bits through on-access pruning: 110 s instead of 32.6, every heap block dirtied, ~31 GiB of WAL shipped to every standby — after which ANALYZE alone sufficed. A plain VACUUM after restore on 19rc1 froze every page; on 18.6 it froze none. *(Nikolay Samokhvalov · postgres.ai)*
- **[OrioleDB public beta](https://supabase.com/blog/orioledb-public-beta)** and **[DBArena](https://supabase.com/blog/introducing-dbarena)** — the table access method (undo log instead of dead tuples, 64-bit XIDs for its tables, index-organized tables, no buffer mapping, copy-on-write checkpoints with row-level WAL) is now a public beta on OrioleDB beta19 over Postgres 17.11, explicitly not for production. The numbers come from DBArena, a Supabase-funded public benchmark of hosted Postgres (Supabase heap, Supabase + OrioleDB, RDS, Cloud SQL; seven tiers from 2 vCPU/2 GiB to 32 vCPU/128 GiB; TPC-C-derived; 30-min warm-up, 60-min runs, median of three fresh environments; 62 results from 750+ runs with a `result.json` per run and open tooling). With data at 4× RAM, OrioleDB had the best tpm/$ at every size and ~1.8× heap's throughput at 8XL (113,850 tpm); in memory the gap is +13% at Large and heap wins at Small. Limits: no PITR, no CLUSTER/VACUUM FULL, non-btree indexes through an "experimental bridge", SERIALIZABLE takes a table lock by default. *(Aditya Maruvada, Daniel Mitterdorfer, Alexander Korotkov, Artur Zakirov · supabase.com)* `[vendor blog — substantive]` `[unverified: vendor-sponsored benchmark]`
- **[Adaptive Integer Compression in TimescaleDB](https://www.tigerdata.com/blog/adaptive-integer-compression-aic-in-timescaledb)** — DeltaDelta + Simple8bRLE assumed one smooth sensor stream; on three interleaved streams it spends 7.49 bytes per value (94% of raw). AIC picks one of six encodings per block of ≤256 values (FOR, PFOR, strided-delta, DICT, RLE, DELTA_RLE) with a SIMD analyser and a tiered FastLanes bit-packer (8–256-bit virtual registers, since batches are ≤1,000 values): 1.88 bytes per value on the same example, integer columns ~50% smaller, decompression 5–7× faster. A detail worth stealing: the smallest AIC blob is under 24 bytes (`MAXALIGN(TOAST_POINTER_SIZE)`), so it stays in the heap row where DeltaDelta's 40-byte minimum was TOASTed — a filtered count went 52.5 → 11.9 ms, 56% of it the TOAST fetch that no longer happens. Off by default in 2.31.0 (`timescaledb.enable_aic_compression`). *(David Beck · tigerdata.com)* `[vendor blog — substantive]`
- **[Designing a planned switchover for minimum disruption](https://planetscale.com/blog/designing-a-planned-switchover-for-minimum-disruption)** — how Neki moves a Postgres primary without a clean shutdown: routers buffer primary-bound queries, the primary gets `default_transaction_read_only`, its LSN is sampled, a candidate that has flushed past it is picked, the *other* replicas are disconnected and their final receive LSNs recorded, and the candidate replays to the maximum. Synchronous replication is `ANY 1 (…)`, so once the others are gone nothing can be acknowledged without the candidate; after promotion at least one replica must rejoin the sync set or the switchover rolls back by reloading the old primary read-write. *(Shlomi Noach · planetscale.com)* `[vendor blog — substantive]`
- **[Splitting the read-only transaction: replica reads inside an open transaction](https://awide.tech/blog/splitting-read-only-transaction)** — Polar Proxy keeps the transaction open on the primary but sends eligible SELECTs to a replica: READ COMMITTED only, top-level, no locking clause, no SET before the first write, and only when the backend reports no unflushed WAL of its own. A patched libpq appends a marker byte to ReadyForQuery — `x` splittable plus the transaction's XID list, `w` WAL pending — at zero extra round trips (compare CF #7415 from the 10-12 issue); the replica gets `SET polar_xact_split_xids`, a strict consistency mode and a wait-for-LSN before the query, and stays pinned for the transaction. No performance numbers. *(Vadim Iatsenko · awide.tech)* `[vendor blog — substantive]`
    - **[Zero-copy I/O for the shared storage daemon](https://awide.tech/blog/zero-copy-pfsd-io):** the whole main shared-memory segment is backed by one `memfd` mapped MAP_SHARED before fork and registered with pfsd once, so WAL buffers, shared buffers and SLRU pages are written in place instead of copied into the daemon's pool; misaligned pointers fall back to the copy

## PostgreSQL mailing lists

- **[hackers] [Racing in ROLLBACK PREPARED and checkpoint](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CAGRkXqRhpq%3Dfd8Qf_-Ki1OW7b1iL%2Bo3YuHiHQhLw5RUJy2JGXQ%40mail.gmail.com)** — `RecordTransactionAbortPrepared()` never set `DELAY_CHKPT_START` (the commit path does). If a checkpoint's redo pointer lands between the abort record and the removal of the 2PC state file, and the host crashes before that removal is durable, recovery finds the file and **the transaction is prepared again; COMMIT PREPARED then succeeds**, so the WAL carries both a ROLLBACK and a COMMIT for one xid — a standby that replayed the abort trips an assertion in clog.c or silently marks it committed. Michael Paquier (Nov 2): at least as old as 2PC itself (2005), agrees with the fix, back-patch to 14. The same WAL-before-file pattern in three more places, from the same reporter: *(Shihao Zhong; Michael Paquier · pgsql-hackers)* `[patch posted]`
    - **[multixact creation](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CAGRkXqSuiJ_X4%2BZ6rsD42pcuACnD9PhoQ-Os%2BmUJNmJarBWkNg%40mail.gmail.com):** the CREATE_ID record is inserted before the SLRU pages are filled; a checkpoint in between and a crash leave a multixact with no members — "the old row version becomes visible again next to the new one" on a non-assert build. 15+; 14 needs the old `delayChkpt` flag `[open]`
    - **[CLOG truncation and hash bucket allocation](https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/CAGRkXqTSAb-H4oxk%3DJ9QBv-Kh2-o%3DcnCWuiSduMVaVzrKYmKXQ%40mail.gmail.com):** `vac_truncate_clog()` truncates before advancing `oldestXid`, so after a crash `pg_xact_status()` on a removed XID errors instead of returning NULL and the next VACUUM does not repair it; `_hash_alloc_buckets()` logs the new splitpoint before extending the file, so a base backup taken in between yields a hash index that works until it needs an overflow page, then fails every insert with "access to noncontiguous page" until REINDEX. ## Research & cutting edge

- **[Synchronized Rebuilds: the hidden cost of concurrent inserts in learned indexes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33770)** — 4,000+ runs across 13 learned indexes: gapped-array designs rebuild nodes in synchronized bursts predictable from three constants in the source, each copying a node ~1,000× larger than a B-tree page and blocking every operation that reaches it; **three of five concurrent learned indexes lost keys or stopped making progress**, which the standard benchmark that rates one "almost ready for use" never notices. A rebuild-time-only buffer and background rebuilds cut inserts over 100 µs by two orders of magnitude at one thread on 400 M real keys. *(Namuganga, Bindschaedler)* _[paper]_
- **[When the application layer is disposable: putting integrity constraints back in the database](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36418)** — SQL-92 ASSERTIONs never got real support, and LLM agents now issue DML straight at the database, bypassing the layer where invariants lived. "Constraint indexes" represent multi-relation constraints as transactional access paths, implemented as a **PostgreSQL extension**: inheritance hierarchies, folded relationships, cardinality bounds and FDs at low overhead relative to no enforcement and up to 2.41× faster than trigger-based baselines; LLM writers frequently violate unenforced constraints and behave erratically when handed a list of them. *(Kalumin, Deshpande)* _[paper]_
- **[Nemo: efficient caching with index pushdown for disaggregated OLTP databases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33306)** — push the whole B+tree to storage and cache hot *records* at compute, which needs a record cache that serves range queries, a fix for the negative-search penalty (confirming absence remotely) and phantom protection without knowing the storage layout.
